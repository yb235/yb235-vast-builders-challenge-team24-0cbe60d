Teaching a warehouse robot when to yield.

Cosmos watches our warehouse footage and labels every moment. A tiny model learns to make the same call from where people and machines are. W&B keeps the receipts.

Every few seconds, a robot has to choose.

Yield or proceed?

A person steps near a moving cart. Stop, or keep going?

Labels are the bottleneck.

Teaching a robot takes thousands of labeled clips. People labeling video is slow and expensive.

The footage already exists.

Our warehouse cameras are already indexed on VAST. What's missing is the labels.

So we let Cosmos do the labeling, and trained something small enough to run on the robot.

How it learns

Cosmos is the teacher. YOLO is the ruler. A tiny model is the student. W&B is the lab notebook.

Every Cosmos and YOLO call goes through the VSS retrieval API. Nothing calls the GPUs directly.

Does the student match the teacher?

Tested on held-out clips it never saw during training.

Why not just count right answers? Saying 'proceed' every time is often right, and never stops for a person. The balanced score rewards getting both calls right.

    Under the hood

      What this is, and what it isn't.

      • About 240 clips, from only two scenes. One is synthetic. This is a working data engine and a baseline model, not a deployable robot policy.
      • Labels come from Cosmos Reason reading captions, not from humans or pixels. The model learns to agree with Cosmos.
      • YOLO cannot name robots, so geometry treats every non-person box as a machine.

      Next step: re-ingest with a labeling prompt, so labels come from the video itself rather than its captions.
